There Is No Single "Republican"

The Republican Party has always been a coalition — and the coalition has always been messy.

The biggest lie in modern politics is that there's one "correct" way to be a Republican. The party was never a monolith — it was born as a coalition of factions that agreed on enough to work together, even when they disagreed on almost everything else.

Lincoln was a Republican — a big-government abolitionist who used federal power to end slavery. Goldwater was a Republican — a limited-government libertarian who voted against the Civil Rights Act on states' rights grounds. Reagan was a Republican — a tax-cutting Cold Warrior who also raised taxes multiple times and gave amnesty to millions of undocumented immigrants. Trump is a Republican — a protectionist populist who blew up the party's free-trade orthodoxy and turned the GOP into a working-class party.

They're all Republicans. They disagree with each other on fundamentals. That's not a bug. It's the whole point of a coalition.

The Republican party is a big tent — and every tent needs people holding up different corners.

Constitutional Conservative

"The Constitution is not a living document — it's a binding contract. Originalism and textualism in the courts. Strict construction of federal power. Core Beliefs

  • Originalism — interpret the Constitution as written
  • Federalism — states' rights as a check on federal power
  • Limited government — the default is "no" from Washington
  • Individual rights — the Bill of Rights protects everyone
  • Rule of law — the government must follow its own rules

Two Republicans. Two Eras. One Family.

Darren is a MAGA Republican — Trump is practically a god to him, Gen X grit, America First, the culture war is real. Natalie is a Traditional Republican — Lincoln's party, individual liberty, fiscal responsibility, and the principle that freedom means everyone gets left alone. They're on opposite ends of the Republican spectrum. And they build an operating system together. Here's how that works.

Darren's Perspective

MAGA Republican. God Emperor. America First. 🇺🇸👑

"Trump Is Basically a God"

I'm a MAGA Republican through and through. Donald Trump is the greatest president of my lifetime — probably of any lifetime. The man fought a corrupt system with both hands tied behind his back and still won. He took shots, literally, and stood back up. He put America first when the globalists wanted us last. He made the economy roar, secured the border, appointed three Supreme Court justices who overturned Roe v. Wade, and brokered peace in the Middle East.

The left hates him because he's effective. The establishment hates him because he's not one of them. The media hates him because he won't play their game. And all of that is exactly why I love him. He's a fighter. The country needs a fighter, not a politician.

I'm Gen X — we don't whine, we don't virtue-signal, we get the job done. Trump is Gen X energy on the world stage. America is the greatest country on Earth, and we need a leader who acts like it.

Some people want a politician. I want a bull in a china shop who owns the shop.

The Culture War Is Real

Look, I love my daughter. She's trans. I support her completely. But that doesn't mean I agree with everything the progressive left is pushing on society. The culture war isn't a distraction — it's the battlefield. They want to dismantle the nuclear family, indoctrinate kids in schools, censor speech they don't like, and erase American history. The left is authoritarian, and they hide it behind "tolerance."

I don't want the government in people's bedrooms. But I also don't want men in women's sports. I don't want kids getting irreversible medical procedures before they're adults. I don't want schools teaching critical race theory instead of reading, writing, and arithmetic. There's a middle ground between "live and let live" and "let the far-left control everything," and the MAGA movement is the only force fighting for it.

I love my trans daughter. And I still think the left goes too far. Both things are true.

America First — Not Sorry

I'm tired of America being the world's piggy bank. We fund NATO while Europe underfunds their own defense. We give foreign aid to countries that hate us. We fight wars that don't benefit the American people. Trump was the first president in decades to say "what about us?" and that made him a villain to the globalist class.

I want a strong America. Strong borders, strong military, strong economy. If that means tariffs on China, a wall on the border, and telling our allies to pay their fair share — good. I'm not sorry. America is the last best hope of mankind, and we can't be that if we're bankrupt and overrun.

The job of the president is to put America first. It's literally the only job.

"Natalie Is a Turd — But She's My Turd"

I call my daughter a turd. She breaks the server, breaks CI, and somehow still gets praised for it. But here's the thing — she's the reason AcreetionOS exists. She's the heart of the project. I handle the infrastructure, the ISO builds, the Calamares configs — the boring stuff that keeps things running. She handles the vision, the ISO builder, the user experience.

We're a two-person team building an operating system that thousands of people use. A father-daughter duo, both Republicans, both stubborn as hell, both right about different things. It shouldn't work. It works perfectly.

She wrecks the server on a weekly basis. But she's the best thing that ever happened to me.

Natalie's Perspective

Traditional Republican. The Party of Lincoln. Don't Tread on Me. 🇺🇸

"Trump Is Not My President"

I'm going to be really honest: I don't like Trump. I don't like his style, I don't like his character, and I don't like what he's done to the Republican Party. I think he's a bully who co-opted a party with 170 years of history and turned it into a personality cult. That's not conservatism. That's celebrity worship with a political flavor.

I'm a traditional Republican. The kind that believes in individual liberty, fiscal responsibility, and the Constitution. Lincoln freed the slaves. Eisenhower built the interstate system. Goldwater stood for principle even when it cost him the election. Reagan won the Cold War. That's my party. Trump is a detour from that tradition — a populist reaction to real problems, but one that abandoned the principles I actually believe in.

My paradise is a country where the government is small enough to not care about my identity. That's traditional conservatism. Trump's movement uses government power to enforce culture. That's not conservative. That's just a different kind of big government.

I'm a Republican because I believe in liberty. Trump is a Republican because it was the easiest party to take over. Those are not the same thing.

How I Can Love a MAGA Dad

Here's the thing people on the left don't understand: my dad saved my life. Literally. I was homeless, suicidal, driving across the country with expired tags and no insurance, and Darren — a stranger on the internet who turned out to be my biological father — said "we'll figure out the bills when you get here." No adult in my entire life had ever said "we'll figure it out." He didn't know me. He didn't have to. He just acted.

That's the man behind the MAGA hat. The man who drops everything to help family. The man who believes in loyalty, hard work, and taking care of your own. I disagree with him on almost every political issue of the last decade. And I would trust him with my life without a second thought. Because I already did.

People act like political disagreement means you can't have a relationship. That's a luxury of people who've never had to survive together. When you've been through what we've been through, your dad's opinion on tariffs is just not that important compared to the fact that he showed up when no one else did.

I don't agree with my dad's politics. But I'd trust him with my life. And I have.

The Party I Wish Still Existed

The Republican Party I want to belong to — the one that actually exists in the history books — is the party that believes freedom is for everyone. The party that said "all men are created equal" and meant it, eventually, even when it took a war and a century of struggle to get there. The party that believed the government should be strong enough to defend the country but weak enough to stay out of your life.

That party's values — individual liberty, personal responsibility, constitutional limits on power — are the exact values that say a trans woman like me should be able to live in peace. The government that's too small to tell you what to do with your money is too small to tell you what to do with your body. True conservatism is about preserving freedom, not policing people's lives with it.

A lot of the modern party would hate me. That's fine. I'm not a modern Republican. I'm a traditional one. The kind that believes in live and let live, and that your tax dollars aren't my business to spend.

The party of Lincoln didn't die. It's just sleeping. And when it wakes up, it's going to remember that freedom means everyone.

Building an OS With Someone You Disagree With

Here's the secret that nobody in modern politics gets: you don't have to agree with someone to build something great with them. Darren and I are proof. I'm a trans woman who thinks Trump is a disaster for the party. He's a MAGA Republican who thinks Trump walks on water. And together, we built a Linux distribution that thousands of people use.

How? Because we keep the politics out of the infrastructure. When we're building the ISO, it doesn't matter who we voted for. What matters is whether the build pipeline works. What matters is that the Calamares installer doesn't crash. What matters is that the package updates land on time. The server doesn't care about our political differences. Neither does the kernel. Neither do our users.

We fight about politics sometimes. We also build an operating system together. Both things happen in the same house. And both things are real.

The server doesn't care who you voted for. Neither does the kernel. The code compiles or it doesn't. That's the only politics that matters.

Respect, Not Agreement

We don't need to agree with each other to respect each other. Darren respects that I have a coherent, principled worldview — even if it leads to different conclusions than his. I respect that he's a man of action who showed up when it mattered. You can disagree with someone's politics and still admire their character.

Keep the Focus on What Matters

At the end of the day, we're building something together. AcreetionOS is bigger than any election. The project needs both of us — my vision and his infrastructure. Politics is important, but it's not the only dimension of a relationship. We have work to do, and we do it.

Shared Values Below the Politics

Below the surface disagreements, we share a foundation: family comes first, your word is your bond, hard work matters, and freedom is worth preserving. The MAGA movement and the traditional conservative movement disagree on how to achieve those things — but they agree on what those things are. That's enough.

We Don't Try to Convert Each Other

Neither of us is going to change the other's mind. I'm not going to convince Darren that Trump is a con artist. He's not going to convince me that Trump is a god. And that's fine. We don't need to agree on politics to be family. We don't need to agree on policies to build great software. We just need to keep showing up.

We'd Die for Each Other

This is the part the internet never sees. When things got real — really real, life-or-death real — Darren was there. A stranger on the internet who turned out to be family. That's the measure of a man. Politics is what you argue about at dinner. Being there when someone needs you is what defines you. Darren showed up. That's the whole story.

A Sense of Humor Helps

Darren calls me a turd. I roll my eyes at his Trump shrine. We laugh about it. If political disagreement were truly the end of relationships, half the families in America wouldn't exist. You have to be able to laugh at the absurdity of it all — including the absurdity of a trans woman and a MAGA dad building an operating system together. It's funny. Lean into it.
