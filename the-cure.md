# The Cure — Root Cause Resolution

## First: the alone

People ask why I'm so comfortable alone. Why I don't reach out. Why I don't seem to need anyone. Let me tell you the whole truth of it, because it's not a flex and it's not a cry for help — it's just what the system made.

I was left. Over and over. Nine foster homes before I was old enough for kindergarten. Pulled from homes I thought were mine — one day a room and a birthday party, the next day a caseworker with a clipboard telling me to pack. Separated from Thomas for his own good. Separated from an adoptive family to save my own life. Left in a corner while the adults were busy being adults. Left to my own in my bedroom — the only place nobody could corner me, forget me, or give me a reason to cry.

Eventually — and this is the part that sounds sad but isn't, not anymore — **I started enjoying it.** The being alone. Actually appreciating it.

It's weird. I know it's weird. But here's the thing: when every human connection you've ever had has been a door that eventually closes, you stop leaning on doors. You stop expecting the person in the room to stay. And then one day you realize the room isn't empty — it's *yours*. Nobody can leave it. Nobody can take it. The quiet stops being loneliness and becomes *privacy*. The solitude stops being punishment and becomes *peace*.

So now people are just... there. Others are just there. Okay cool. There are a few I genuinely care about — a very few, and they know who they are, and they're the ones who stayed when staying was hard. But everyone else? **They could disappear and I wouldn't care.** Lol. I mean that literally. I've watched people walk out of my life my whole life — family, friends, doctors, employers, whole institutions — and I've learned that the ones who leave were never going to stay anyway. So why would I spend the energy caring about people who are just... passing through?

I hate crowds now. The noise, the bodies, the *expectation* of social performance — all of it. I find people exhausting, if I'm honest. Because loving people is the thing that's hurt me the most, and I've never been good at doing it in moderation. So I keep the few, I tolerate the many, and I treasure the alone. The alone is the only relationship that has never once left me.

That's not a tragedy. That's a *survival adaptation* — the same way a scar is. It's what's left after being left enough times to learn the shape of it. And it's the foundation everything below was built on: when the system abandoned me, I was already an expert at being alone. So I did what I'd always done. I fixed it myself.
