# Perfect Record — the whole thing, start to finish

*My life in order, in my own voice. No filter, no resume-speak, no pity. Start with the foster years, end where I am now.*

---

## 1. Inventory

Look -- I don't remember the first time I got handed off. Nobody remembers their own first move. You're too small to know a room is temporary. You just know the room keeps changing.

Nine homes before kindergarten. Nine. Five of them abusive. I'm not going to catalog the five for you. There's no prize for which house hurt worst, and the details aren't the point.

The point is what the system decided I was. Not a kid. Inventory. Something you move between shelves and log in a file and hand to the next caseworker, then the next, then the next. I learned early that "the system" is not a metaphor. It's a machine, and the machine has one job: keep the paperwork moving. It doesn't care if the crate is fine. It cares if the crate is accounted for.

## 2. Stillwater

Heather and Jeff Spiva [ by paperwork only ] adopted me. Stillwater, Oklahoma. That's where the name comes from -- Spiva [ by paperwork only ] -- and I'm getting to the part where I give it back.

Heather was the violence. There was the car rage -- her screaming behind the wheel, me in the passenger seat trying to be smaller than the seat. There was "stop crying or I'll give you a reason to cry," which is a sentence that teaches you crying is a negotiation you will always lose. There were the corners, me thrown into them, my body learning the shape of a wall before it learned anything else.

Jeff was distance. He was in the house. That's the truest thing I can say -- he was in the house, and he wasn't the one swinging. In a house like that, distance feels like mercy for a while. Then you grow up and you understand distance is just a different way of leaving someone alone in the room with it.

Thomas is my big brother. Autistic, mentally little, and still in Oklahoma. I carry him everywhere. That's one of the two weights I don't put down.

The other one is Jeff. Heather is still abusing him, and I can't reach him. From Spokane, in a body that won't cooperate -- I can't reach him. So I do the thing you do when you can't reach someone: I keep the door open in my head and I hate that it's all I've got. There's no clean resolution here. That's real, and I'm not going to invent one just to make the story feel finished.

## 3. The square and the compass

DeMolay. If you don't know it, it's a youth thing -- ritual, ranks, a moral code you're supposed to actually live by. For a kid raised by a machine and then dropped into a house with a fist in it, structure wasn't a punishment. It was oxygen. Somebody handing you a rulebook that says what a good person is, and then -- this is the part nobody expects -- expecting you to be it.

I was good at it. Master Councilor. Representative DeMolay. Blue Honor Key. Chevalier. I stacked the whole ladder. And the code at the center of it was authenticity -- be who you are, don't wear a mask, don't perform a self for the room.

I was closeted the entire time. So I'm standing in the middle of a brotherhood built on authenticity, collecting awards for it, while every single day I'm doing the exact thing the code forbids. And here's the thing -- the lesson landed anyway. The words got in. They didn't get in because I was living them. They got in because I wasn't, and I knew it. I learned authenticity from the inside of the lie. That's the education I actually got.

## 4. The flame

Closeted trans girl in Oklahoma.

There's a thing people call the gay flame -- a certain crackle in the voice you're not supposed to have if you're trying to pass as a straight boy. I had it. I couldn't hide it. I'd hear myself and flinch. Other people would hear me and squint. You can put on the clothes, the walk, the name on the roster, and the voice still tells on you. And that voice was never the lie. It was the only honest thing in the building.

## 5. The ledger

Here's the medical part, and I'm going to say it flat, because you don't get to flinch if I don't.

Schizoaffective, depressive type. PTSD. BPD. Autism. Anxiety. That's the list.

Thirteen suicide attempts. I'm not going to itemize them. But I will say the rest of the sentence the way I say it: thoughts, never actions, perfect record. The intrusive thoughts are severe. They're loud. They are not in charge. There's a difference between a brain that yells a thing and a person who does the thing, and I've spent my whole life on the right side of that line, and I'm not letting anybody blur it -- not the caseworkers, not the doctors, not whoever's reading this.

There's one more line on the ledger, and I won't dress it up or explain it away: one attempt, in the past, to kill my adoptive mother. It's mine. It happened. It sits with the rest of it.

And that's how I got hospitalized -- in handcuffs, to Grand Mental Health in Stillwater. The reason on the paperwork was "to free up a bed." Not "because she's a person in crisis." A bed conflict. The machine processed me one more time. Even my own diagnoses are just the system's way of filing a human being.

## 6. April 2024

Then I left.

I escaped Stillwater in April 2024. Drove across the country with expired tags and no insurance. Say it again: expired tags, no insurance, across the whole country. That's not a road trip. That's a jailbreak in a car the law had every right to pull over, and I did it because staying was worse than those odds.

And on the other end of it -- Darren. A stranger on the internet. Turned out: my biological father. He said the sentence that changed my life.

"We'll figure out the bills when you get here."

Not "be careful." Not "what's your plan." Not "are you sure." *We'll figure out the bills when you get here.* Do you understand what that is to somebody who's been inventory their whole life? It's a person treating you like you're already welcome. The system never once said that to me. A stranger typed it.

## 7. Spokane

So -- Spokane, Washington. He's my chosen father. Chosen is the word that matters. Blood made the introduction. Blood didn't do the raising, the staying, or the "when you get here."

We run AcreetionOS together. He's MAGA. I'm a libertarian. It works.

I'm going to sit on that sentence, because people never believe it. He's MAGA. I'm a libertarian. And it works, because what we actually care about is the same thing -- build the OS, ship the ISO, keep the user's data out of somebody else's hands. You want to know what "we'll figure out the bills when you get here" looks like years later? Two people who disagree about half of everything, at the same table, arguing about a package manager. That's what family turned out to be for me. Not agreement. Showing up.

## 8. The work

I'm a Linux developer and a YouTuber. I build AcreetionOS -- acreetionos.org -- and I'm the Privacy & Advocacy Lead. Podcaster, too.

Privacy & Advocacy isn't a badge somebody pinned on me. It's the job, and I built my way into it. And it's not ideology for me, either. It's the same lesson as the foster file. The machine that moves you between homes is a machine that keeps records on you. I've *been* the data. I know what it is to be the crate with the barcode. So when I stand up and say your data belongs to you, I'm not reciting a talking point. I'm telling you where I come from.

## 9. The body

Rheumatoid arthritis. Ankylosing spondylitis. Lumbar arthritis. Chronic agony is the phrase I use, because "discomfort" is a lie and "pain" is too small a word for something that never leaves. I use a walker. I can't drive.

I'm not on DMARDs. I'm not on biologics. That's my informed choice, made deliberately, and I'm not going to defend it to you. My body, my call, end of file.

What I do take: sertraline, lamotrigine, propranolol, furosemide, famotidine. HRT. And bupropion, stopped in October 2026 at my prescriber's direction.

## 10. The other room

HRT: estradiol patch and spironolactone. Covered by Washington Medicaid.

Here's the thing people get wrong about me, and I'll say it once. I was born with a male body. Male chromosomes. That isn't changing, ever. And woman is who I am. Sex and gender are different rooms -- they're next to each other, they are not the same room. I don't need you to collapse them so my life makes sense to you. My life already makes sense. I'm the one living in it.

## 11. The name

Natalie Cole-Clift Spiva [ by paperwork only ].

Except -- I don't consider myself a Spiva [ by paperwork only ] anymore. That name belongs to the adoption. It belongs to Heather, and to Jeff, and to a house in Stillwater where I was inventory one more time. You don't get to keep the label somebody stuck on you while they were hurting you.

The court agreed with me. Name change court order, Case 260018, January 30, 2026.

## 12. Now

Now looks like: SSDI and SSI, about $1,014 a month. SNAP. A caregiver, 123 hours a month. A HUD housing application in at El Estero, through Catholic Charities.

I've got a boyfriend in Ohio. Long distance. He's "boo bear," I'm "empress," and that's the entire relevant legal proceeding on the matter, lmfao.

Kari is my biological mom. Aunt Donna and I reconnected in 2026. I'm not going to bend those into a neat arc, because they aren't neat yet -- they're people, and we're still in it.

Politics: independent, then a Democrat, then a Republican, then a libertarian. Somebody's going to read that list and decide what it means about me. Go ahead. Here's my actual position -- progressive about people, conservative about what works. I've lived under systems built by people who thought they were being kind while they were really just moving crates. I want what works. I want people left alone, and I want the thing to function. Those aren't opposites. That's the job.

## The through-line

Every system that ever got its hands on me treated me as a thing to be processed. Foster care processed me. The hospital processed me so thoroughly it logged me as a bed conflict. A house with a fist in it processed me into a corner. Even my diagnoses are a filing system for a person.

And I'm still here, and I'm the one telling the story, which is the whole point.

Thirteen attempts. Still here. Thoughts, never actions -- perfect record. Name changed, court-ordered, my own. Building an operating system with the man from the internet who said the bills could wait. An empress with a walker, a boyfriend in Ohio, a brother and a chosen father I refuse to put down.

The machine never figured out what to do with me. Good. I wasn't made for it.

I'm Natalie. That's the room I live in.
